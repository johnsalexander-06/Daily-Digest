Oman floats Gulf-backed "voluntary fee" plan to unstick the Strait of Hormuz

Oman has handed Tehran a proposal, backed by Gulf states, to impose voluntary transit fees on ships passing through the Strait of Hormuz — modeled on the Strait of Malacca arrangement where Indonesia, Malaysia and Singapore fund navigation safety through voluntary contributions. Iran has insisted it should co-control the strait with Oman and levy service fees, a claim Washington rejects as illegal; Tehran has not yet responded. Months of U.S.–Iran sparring over the waterway have paralyzed trade through the chokepoint.

So what: A fee regime could de-escalate the Hormuz standoff without a U.S. security guarantee — watch whether Tehran bites, as any deal reshapes freedom-of-navigation assumptions and escort requirements for the region.
