Breaking
Russia unleashes large-scale overnight strike series across Ukraine; at least 8 dead
On the night of July 29–30, Russian forces launched roughly 284 Shahed/Gerbera/Italmas-type drones and decoys plus 74 missiles — including Zircon/Onyx anti-ship, Iskander-M/S-400/KN-23 ballistic, and Kh-101/Kalibr cruise missiles — mostly against Kyiv and Lviv oblasts. Ukraine intercepted the majority, but strikes hit 20 locations across at least eleven oblasts. A ballistic missile in Radushne, Dnipropetrovsk destroyed a residential building and killed two parents and four of their children; Zelensky reported at least eight dead and dozens injured. Russia continues shifting toward more ballistic missiles to exploit Ukraine's interceptor shortage.

AFA Daily Report · Jul 31

Air Force says Collaborative Combat Aircraft will be "optionally attritable"

The Air Force's new CCAs may fly scores of missions over many years, or be spent on only a few if a commander judges the objective worth losing the aircraft, a senior official said this week. The "optionally attritable" framing stakes out a middle ground between fully expendable munitions and exquisite manned platforms, keeping loss decisions in the hands of operational commanders on a mission-by-mission basis.

So What: The attrition-versus-value call for autonomous wingmen will sit with commanders, not the acquisition system — build CCA loss authorities, ROE, and replacement-flow assumptions into force-package and campaign planning now.
AFA Daily Report · Jul 31

Former NORAD chief: US air bases are vulnerable to a Ukraine-style drone raid

Retired Gen. Glen VanHerck, ex-head of U.S. Northern Command and NORAD, warned July 30 that heavily guarded bases inside the United States could still fall victim to the kind of close-in drone attack that struck bomber bases deep inside Russia last year. The comments underscore that low-cost, short-range unmanned systems now threaten high-value aircraft on the ramp far from any declared theater.

So What: Rear-area base defense is now a homeland problem, not just a deployed one — reassess installation counter-UAS coverage, perimeter ISR, and dispersal/sheltering for high-value aircraft.
AFA Daily Report · Jul 31

Space Force eyes "usable" airborne moving-target tracking from orbit by 2028

The Space Force expects its nascent space-based airborne moving-target indicator (AMTI) program to reach a "usable capability" within the next few years, with additional work on sensing, data processing, and orchestration needed before it delivers global targeting from space. The effort aims to move a mission long tied to vulnerable airborne platforms into orbit.

So What: A space-based GMTI/AMTI layer would cut reliance on JSTARS-type aircraft in contested airspace — plan for orbital ISR feeds entering the targeting cycle later this decade and the C2 to exploit them.

So What: Interceptor scarcity forces hard cross-theater allocation choices — assume constrained air-and-missile-defense resupply and refine defended-asset priority lists before the next surge.
