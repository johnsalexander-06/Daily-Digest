Air & Space Forces Magazine · Aug 4

F-22s deploy to Kadena as 18th Wing still waits on its F-15EX

Two rotational fifth-generation fighter squadrons are running a readiness exercise at Kadena Air Base, Japan, while the 18th Wing continues to wait for the permanently stationed F-15EX fighters meant to replace its retired F-15C/D fleet. The rotational-fighter model has kept a fifth-gen presence over the first island chain but leaves Okinawa without a resident air-superiority squadron during a period of heightened PRC activity. Delivery slips on the F-15EX are stretching the gap between the legacy drawdown and the modern replacement.

So what: The theater's most contested main operating base is riding on rotational augmentation, not organic capability — assume Kadena's steady-state air-superiority posture stays thin into 2027 and plan ACE dispersal and tanker support accordingly.

Air Force wants cheap, attritable MQ-9 replacements — vendors picked by year's end

The Air Force plans to select vendors by the end of 2026 to develop concepts for low-cost, attritable drones that would take over the MQ-9 Reaper's persistent ISR and strike mission in a new way. The Reaper is survivable in permissive skies but not against a peer integrated air-defense system, and the service wants a family of expendable platforms rather than a single exquisite airframe. The move signals a deliberate shift from small numbers of high-value RPAs toward mass and affordability.

So what: The ISR/strike force mix your planning assumes today is being redesigned around attritable mass — start pressure-testing CONOPS that trade a few exquisite orbits for many cheaper ones, and expect force-structure turbulence in the RPA community.
Breaking Defense · Aug 4

K2 tapped for Space Force satellite laser-link tests underpinning Golden Dome

The Space Force selected K2 to host tests of satellite-to-satellite optical laser-light communications, the backbone of its future missile-warning and missile-tracking architecture. Those crosslinks are central to the Trump administration's Golden Dome missile-defense shield, which depends on a proliferated space layer passing track data at machine speed. Maturing optical links is the technical long pole for fusing space-based sensing into a fire-control-quality picture.

So what: Golden Dome's viability rests on plumbing being tested now, not on the interceptors that get headlines — track optical-crosslink progress as the leading indicator of whether space-based missile tracking arrives on schedule.
Breaking Defense / AFA · Aug 4

Northrop lands $3B framework deal to expand PAC-3 and THAAD motor production

Northrop Grumman will stand up as a second supplier of solid rocket motors for the in-demand PAC-3 interceptor while also boosting motor output for THAAD, under multiyear deals worth a combined $3 billion. Solid rocket motors have been the chokepoint throttling air-and-missile-defense interceptor deliveries, and a second qualified source directly attacks that bottleneck. Demand has surged as Patriot and THAAD batteries are drawn down defending Ukraine, Israel, and Gulf partners.

So what: Interceptor magazine depth is a readiness ceiling for every air-defense-dependent operation — a second motor source eases it, but the relief is years out, so treat PAC-3/THAAD inventory as a constrained resource through this planning cycle.

Russia revives its air-interdiction playbook to isolate Ukraine's Fortress Belt

Russian forces are replicating the 2025 battlefield air-interdiction (BAI) campaign — drones, missiles, and glide bombs against rear-area logistics — now aimed at Slovyansk and Kramatorsk, the northern anchor of Ukraine's Fortress Belt in Donetsk Oblast. Moscow is hunting Ukrainian locomotives and just struck a heavy-vehicle bridge over the Kazennyi Torets River linking Slovyansk to Kramatorsk, and reportedly issued troops 1,000 Molniya loitering munitions in July with orders to intensify logistics strikes. The objective is to sever sustained resupply and shape the battlefield before a future ground push, though ISW judges a rapid advance through the cities unlikely near-term.

So what: Russia is telegraphing its next major axis by attacking logistics first — the tempo of bridge and rail strikes around Slovyansk-Kramatorsk is the indicator to watch for when the ground offensive begins.
