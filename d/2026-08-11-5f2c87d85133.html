Lockheed's Next Generation Interceptor clears two key tests ahead of design review

Lockheed Martin's Next Generation Interceptor — the homeland missile-defense weapon meant to supersede the troubled Ground-Based Midcourse Defense interceptor — passed two major test milestones this week and is on track to clear its critical design review later this year, company officials said. The program had absorbed an 18-month delay tied to rocket-motor problems. NGI is the interceptor layer intended to defend the continental U.S. against a limited ICBM strike from North Korea or Iran.

So WhatNGI staying on its 2026 design-review timeline is the pacing item for homeland ballistic-missile defense credibility. Commanders tied to strategic deterrence or Golden Dome planning should treat the schedule as firming and validate their fielding assumptions against it.

Boeing to sell drone maker Insitu and two aviation units to eVTOL firm Archer

Boeing agreed to divest three subsidiaries — uncrewed-aircraft maker Insitu (ScanEagle, RQ-21), air-traffic-management firm SkyGrid, and electric-aircraft developer Wisk Aero — to Archer Aviation, with the sales expected to close by year's end pending regulatory approval. So WhatA change in ownership of a fielded tactical-ISR drone line can ripple into sustainment, spares, and contract continuity. Units operating ScanEagle/Insitu systems should confirm support arrangements and watch for program-office guidance on the transition.

The Marine Corps awarded Epirus $11 million for its HAVOC high-power-microwave system, which will feature a "universal sled mount" so it can be paired with a range of manned and unmanned vehicles across the service. So WhatMobile high-power-microwave counter-UAS is maturing from prototype toward fielded kit as drone-swarm threats grow. Force-protection and air-defense planners should factor emerging HPM options into layered counter-drone concepts.

For half an hour on Aug. 10, mobile data was deliberately throttled across central Taiwan, leaving millions temporarily unable to stream, share, or make video calls — the island's first mass-scale test of communications resilience under a simulated Chinese disruption. Institute for the Study of War · Aug 10

Russia reprioritizes ballistic missiles and jet-powered drones to exploit Ukraine's Patriot shortage

Ukrainian military intelligence says Russia has been reprioritizing ballistic and cruise missile production since April 2026 to exploit Ukraine's shortage of Patriot interceptors, more than tripling monthly ballistic-missile launches from April to July. Moscow is also shifting drone output toward faster jet-powered Geran-4 and Geran-5 variants that shorten Ukraine's interception window, planning roughly 11,000 strike drones in August alone. Patriots remain Ukraine's only system able to intercept Russian ballistic missiles, and ISW assesses the reduced interceptor supply will help Russia protract the war into 2027.

So WhatRussia is deliberately timing a mass precision-strike campaign to the interceptor gap ahead of winter, betting it can break Ukrainian will rather than negotiate. EUCOM planners should treat Patriot and interceptor resupply as the decisive sustainment variable this winter and press allied stockpile solutions.

Moldova blamed Russia for a weekend airspace violation after a "missile-like" drone crashed and exploded in the village of Crocmaz, damaging property but causing no casualties, and recalled its ambassador from Moscow for consultations. OpenAI pauses its Astra model after evaluations neared "Critical" cyber capability

OpenAI paused work on its upcoming Astra model after internal evaluations suggested it could approach "Critical" cybersecurity capability under the company's own risk framework — a threshold implying the model could meaningfully aid offensive cyber operations.
