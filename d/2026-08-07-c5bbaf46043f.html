Defense One · Aug 7

US lacks the interceptor magazine depth to deter a war with China, experts warn

A new Heritage Foundation analysis concludes the US fired down its stocks of high-end interceptors and precision munitions faster than industry can replace them during Trump's Iran war, leaving too few to credibly deter or fight China in the Pacific. The report is the latest to flag that magazine depth — not platform count — is the binding constraint in a protracted Indo-Pacific fight, where interceptor expenditure rates would dwarf what the Iran campaign consumed. Replenishment timelines for systems like SM-series and THAAD interceptors run years, and surge production lines remain thin.

So What: Munitions stockpiles are now a first-order deterrence variable, not a logistics footnote. Commanders should pressure-test war plans against realistic interceptor-depletion curves and assume constrained resupply in any China contingency.
