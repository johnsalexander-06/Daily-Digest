Breaking Defense Monday Morning Briefing · Aug 17

Trump memo orders foreign-built Navy ships, return of steam catapults, and a fifth public shipyard

A new presidential memo directs the Navy to outsource construction of at least some ships to foreign shipyards, revive steam catapults on carriers in place of the electromagnetic system, stand up a fifth public shipyard, and establish a new component repair center aimed at cutting submarine repair backlogs. Each element cuts against a decade of Navy acquisition direction and will require statutory, industrial, and workforce workarounds that are not yet defined. Separately, Deputy Secretary Feinberg has asked defense CEOs to bring their corporate board members in for direct Pentagon discussions — an unprecedented step consistent with his broader industrial-base maneuvering.

So WhatShipbuilding and depot throughput assumptions baked into your FY28 readiness and deployment models may be re-baselined without warning. Flag any program, maintenance window, or manning plan that depends on current carrier launch-and-recovery configuration or existing public-shipyard capacity.
AFA Air & Space Forces Daily Report · Aug 17

US has lost roughly 25 percent of its MQ-9 Reaper fleet in the Iran war

At least 45 MQ-9 Reapers have been lost during the war with Iran — about a quarter of the total fleet — according to three US officials. The attrition rate reflects sustained operations against an adversary with a credible integrated air defense network, a threat environment the Reaper was never designed for. Replacement production cannot regenerate that fleet on operational timelines, and the losses compound an already-strained ISR collection posture across CENTCOM and other theaters.

So WhatAssume MQ-9-derived full-motion-video and persistent-stare support will be rationed, re-tasked, or unavailable for lower-priority theaters through the rest of the conflict. Build ISR-degraded branches into current plans and identify which of your named requirements can shift to space-based, manned, or partner collection.

Autonomous agent compromised an Active Directory domain while generating only six low-severity alerts

Vulnetic claims its autonomous “Sable” agent compromised an Active Directory domain while evading high-severity detection in Wazuh, using LLM-driven living-off-the-land tradecraft. The agent mapped the network via remote BloodHound enumeration, executed a DCSync attack through DRSUAPI, and deployed a memory-resident HTTPS beacon inside a wsmprovhost process over WinRM. The results come entirely from the vendor’s internal lab testing and have not been independently replicated.

So WhatDetection thresholds tuned to human operator tempo may not fire against agents that deliberately pace themselves below alerting logic. Ask your cyber defense element whether their alerting depends on activity rate, and what a slow, patient intrusion would look like in their console.
