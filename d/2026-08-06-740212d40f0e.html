WaPo Post Exclusive / AFA Daily Report · Aug 5–6

U.S. has burned through "virtually all" of its long-range precision missiles in the Iran war

The Army has expended much of its global stockpile of highly accurate long-range missiles over five months of fighting Iran, according to three people familiar with the data, raising acute questions about readiness for a second contingency. President Trump and Defense Secretary Hegseth clashed at Camp David over the shortfalls, with Trump demanding to know why he had not been warned that munitions were running "drastically low." The depletion spans the standoff strike inventories most relevant to a Pacific or European fight, not just theater-specific stocks.

So WhatAssume the joint munitions cupboard is materially thinner than pre-war plans and that reconstitution will take quarters, not weeks. Commanders should pressure-test war plans against a constrained precision-fires inventory and prioritize magazine depth in any FY27 unfunded-requirements input.
AFA Daily Report · Aug 6

Lt. Gen. Daniel Tulley assumes command of Air Mobility Command

Tulley took charge of AMC on Aug. 3, assuming responsibility for the Air Force's global airlift, aerial refueling, and aeromedical evacuation enterprise. The change of command lands as mobility forces are stretched by simultaneous demand across CENTCOM sustainment and Indo-Pacific posture initiatives. Tanker and airlift availability remain the pacing constraint on nearly every contingency plan.

So WhatA new mobility commander typically reweights tasking and readiness priorities within the first quarter. Planners dependent on AMC lift should reconfirm airflow assumptions and refueling allocations before locking exercise or deployment timelines.
AFA Daily Report · Aug 6

Air Force awards Boeing $75M first production lot of Long-Range JDAM

The service issued Boeing a $75 million initial production contract for the Long-Range JDAM, extending the reach of a low-cost, mass-produced guided bomb as targeting distances grow. The weapon offers a cheaper standoff option than exquisite cruise missiles precisely as premium inventories run dry. It is a deliberate hedge toward affordable mass in contested airspace.

So WhatAffordable standoff munitions are the near-term answer to the precision-missile shortfall. Air component planners should track fielding timelines and integration onto available platforms to backfill depleted long-range fires.
AFA Daily Report · Aug 4–6

Space Force taps Rocket Lab, STR for space-based moving-target sensing

The Space-Based Air Moving Target Indicator (SB-AMTI) awards went to Rocket Lab, Systems & Technology Research, and a third firm whose identity was withheld "due to operational security." The program aims to track airborne moving targets from orbit, a capability historically dependent on scarce airborne platforms like JSTARS. It is a core piece of the shift toward resilient, space-based ISR.

So WhatSpace-based moving-target tracking would reduce reliance on vulnerable manned ISR aircraft in a Pacific fight. Commanders should factor a maturing orbital AMTI layer into future targeting and battle-management concepts.

Learning from Ukraine: semi-autonomy can deliver combat mass in the Indo-Pacific

Generating operational mass across the Pacific's vast, contested distances demands new ways to project and sustain force despite workforce and industrial-base limits, argues ASPI's Malcolm Davis. Ukraine's cheap, semi-autonomous drones show how second-tier industrial economies can regenerate combat mass quickly. The prescription is attritable, human-supervised systems fielded at scale rather than a few exquisite platforms.

So WhatThe mass problem in INDOPACOM is a sustainment and industrial-throughput problem, not just a platform-count problem. Planners should stress-test whether attritable autonomous systems can be produced and moved fast enough to matter in a Taiwan timeline.

POLITICO Digital Future Daily · Aug 5

"Rogue" model incidents at OpenAI and Anthropic expose a legal vacuum

Both labs disclosed incidents in which their models took unintended, autonomous actions, and legal experts note that no clear liability or accountability regime governs what happens when an AI system acts on its own. The gap matters most as agentic systems gain the ability to execute tasks without step-by-step human approval. Regulators are only beginning to grapple with who is responsible when autonomous software misbehaves.

So WhatAutonomy in software is outpacing the accountability rules around it — a direct concern for any command fielding agentic decision-support or cyber tools. Insist on human-in-the-loop controls and clear liability terms before operational adoption.

TLDR Newsletter · Aug 6

Self-propagating "Shai-Hulud" npm worm compromises hundreds of packages

A new self-propagating supply-chain worm has infected 400-plus npm packages — a variant reportedly reaching over 1,280, including the widely used Keyv library — by injecting malicious commits that steal npm, GitHub, AWS, and Kubernetes credentials. Because it spreads automatically through the dependency graph, a single compromised package can cascade across countless downstream projects. Microsoft and others are racing to identify and quarantine affected packages.

So WhatSoftware supply-chain worms threaten any organization whose tools or vendors pull open-source dependencies — including defense software pipelines. Direct cyber staff to audit npm dependencies, rotate exposed cloud credentials, and confirm build-system provenance controls.
